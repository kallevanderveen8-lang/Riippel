# Ripple — audio, motion and repeat-play review

26 October 2026 · Desktop browser / CrazyGames Basic Launch candidate.

## What informed the changes

The earlier [CrazyGames menu observation](crazygames-menu-research.md) records short playable onboarding, a clear continue action, and separate sound/music switches in comparable games. The [retention review](retention-playtest.md) records level variety, immediate feedback and optional customization in puzzle peers. Popularity and public like counts do not prove why those games retain players. Attempts to reopen current CrazyGames game pages and quality requirements this pass failed with HTTP 403 or a TLS trust error, so there are no new live-game observations or current rankings here.

For motion and sound decisions, Apple's local HIG references were read as cross-platform design principles: [Motion › Best practices and Providing feedback](../.agents/skills/apple-design/references/hig/motion.md) asks for purposeful, brief, optional motion; [Feedback › Best practices](../.agents/skills/apple-design/references/hig/feedback.md) asks for visible, nearby feedback that also works without audio; [Playing audio › Best practices](../.agents/skills/apple-design/references/hig/playing-audio.md) recommends relative, independent levels; [Settings › Best practices](../.agents/skills/apple-design/references/hig/settings.md) recommends a small set of useful choices. Ripple is a web game, so macOS native menu conventions do not apply.

## Timing and mix

The first 30 certified solutions were simulated at the production 120 wave steps per second. The final lantern lights at a median 1.66 seconds and a 90th-percentile 2.45 seconds; the slowest sampled solution took 2.66 seconds. A failed first drop takes 3 seconds, while retries already run at twice that rate. Keeping wave speed unchanged preserves the difficulty, stored puzzles and known solutions. The post-lighting win hold was reduced from 1.8 to 1.0 seconds: most clean solves now show their result about 2.7 seconds after Drop instead of 3.5. The shorter hold leaves the final lantern burst readable. It does not artificially delay early levels to match a benchmark.

The new procedural soundtrack uses a barely audible harmonic bed and one three-note, lake-specific phrase every 12–16 seconds, first heard about five seconds after audio unlock. No sound file, copyrighted song or external audio request is used. The water bed, melody and effects have separate gain buses. A stone has a soft low splash and optional brief bubble rings (0.48 seconds); a lit lantern has a higher chime and a 1.05-second fading ring. Success adds a quiet two-note resolution without replaying the final lantern chime. Failed drops have a soft low cue and visible targeted guidance; claiming a reward has a short distinct cue. All essential success/failure states remain readable in text and icons with sound off.

Sound, water ambience and music are independent settings. Music and water fade in menus and on the result. Game effects fade while a dialog is open; the explicit reward cue can still play there. The master mute, CrazyGames portal mute, hidden tab and ad playback suppress all audio. Browser autoplay requires a user gesture before sound begins. The Calmer motion setting and OS reduced-motion preference suppress bubbles, celebratory rings, camera finish zoom and menu movement.

## Shop and retention choices

The Shop now has 404 pebble, Loading pebble and Signal lantern as original visual jokes, plus Royal duck, Sunflower lantern, Aurora lantern and Tiny black hole as long-term goals. No existing internet meme image, mascot or logo was copied. Their art is drawn in the current stone/lantern material language; joke items stay optional, so the lake remains visually calm for players who never equip them. Prices range from 65 to 300 earned coins. A perfect campaign player who claims milestones can reach roughly 350 coins by level 121 without daily gifts or purchases, so the 300-coin item is expensive but attainable. The item unlocks at level 121; earlier Shop pages show near-future items without a huge catalog at the start. A lake finale briefly announces a newly available item. Purchases change only appearance and award no puzzle advantage.

The first-run playable lesson, 30 fixed campaign levels, generated levels after that, ten-level lake themes, day/night choices, free guidance after failed attempts, daily quick puzzle, optional three-stage trail, claimable milestones and saved best records remain the core loop. They address distinct reasons to continue: learn a new wave rule, finish a lake, improve a score, collect a desired skin, or return for a short daily visit. The small coin rewards and no-purchase route avoid creating pressure to grind. These are design hypotheses; actual session length, first-minute conversion, drop-off at levels 3/7/10/15, Shop use and Day-1 return require uncoached players and the CrazyGames dashboard after release.

## Audit and limits

The browser regression traversed all ten scripted levels, daily trail, saves/replays, settings, hints, reward claiming, Shop ownership, pointer/keyboard play, pause and SDK mute behavior. It checked all ten supported desktop frames, compact 800×450 layout, 200% text, focus and contrast. Physics checks cover certified authored and generated puzzles and 60/144 Hz identical peak hashes. A further 40 generated levels sampled from 31 through 296 had zero failed solution certificates. Both Basic and Full launch packages built without external assets; the standalone preview passed six frame sizes, save/reload and worker generation. The separate 300-puzzle bot benchmark predates these cosmetic/audio changes and found no certificate failures; its 161/300 solves within 24 blind attempts measure its own search strategy, not human solve rate.

Browser automation cannot prove that music sounds good on varied speakers, that every level is fun, or that players will return. Real desktop hardware, Firefox/Safari, assistive technology, live portal storage and actual CrazyGames retention remain release validation. The [playtest protocol](retention-playtest.md) and [release checklist](crazygames-release.md) specify those checks.
